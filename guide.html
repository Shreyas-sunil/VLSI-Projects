Guide

Career tracks

Track A AI Accelerator and GPU Design

Microarchitecture, systolic arrays, dataflow, quantised and sparse compute, memory hierarchy, RTL, FPGA prototyping and PPA.

Track B GPU System Software, CUDA and Firmware

CUDA kernels, profiling, memory hierarchy, runtimes and drivers, boot and firmware, heterogeneous CPU-GPU work.

Track C SoC Architecture

RISC-V cores and custom instructions, AXI and interconnect, DMA, caches, NoC, verification of interfaces, integration and system-level PPA.

Scoring rubric

Each project is scored 1-5 on six criteria (maximum 30). Scores are planning estimates so projects can be compared; they are not measurements. Rank by the quality of the engineering, not by buzzwords in the title.

#CriterionMeaning
1RelevanceHow directly the engineering work maps to the three career tracks. 5 = core skill for the track; 1 = bridge subject with a thin connection.
2Technical depthHow much real engineering understanding is needed. 5 = deep architecture, algorithms or physics; 1 = largely tool use or a direct application.
3Feasibility (low cost)Can it be finished with free tools and a laptop or modest hardware? 5 = fully open tools and CPU only; 1 = needs scarce hardware or heavy compute.
4Measurable evaluationQuality of the baseline and metrics. 5 = clear baseline, numeric metrics, repeatable measurement; 1 = mostly qualitative.
5Portfolio distinctivenessHow memorable the artifact is to a hiring manager. 5 = a verified, runnable hardware or software system; 1 = a document or small script.
6Research potentialCredible route to a workshop or conference paper. 5 = open question with a measurable contribution; 1 = no publishable angle. Most projects are student-level contributions.

Priority labels

  • Build first - a strong foundational or portfolio project.
  • Build next - a substantial intermediate project.
  • Stretch - advanced, resource-intensive or research-oriented.

Project card anatomy

Every card has: identification and syllabus mapping; the engineering problem; the proposed architecture with a module flow; a numbered implementation plan (study, build, artifact, gate); tools and alternatives; baseline, metrics and success criteria; deliverables; career value including a resume bullet and interview questions; risks, fallback and extensions; and the six-part score. Effort is shown in weeks and approximate focused hours assuming about 12 hours per week alongside coursework.

Evidence rules

Claim typeHow to treat it
Established techniquePublished and widely used; cite it.
Proposed design choiceA decision recommended in a project card; justify it when you build.
HypothesisSomething the project is designed to test; do not state it as a result.
Expected outcomeWhat would count as success; a target, not a promise.
Demonstrated resultOnly valid after you run the experiment. Label each result as measured, simulated, analytical estimate or design target.

Simulation, synthesis and place-and-route results are not silicon validation. A literature review shows a problem matters; it does not make your solution novel.

Syllabus audit and discrepancies

Course codes, titles, credits, units and practicals were cross-checked against the 2026 scheme PDF. The PDF itself contains inconsistencies; this site uses the scheme tables and index and notes the differences:

  • Credits: the scheme tables give Semester I 23, II 21, III 19 and IV 7 credits (70 in total). 1 credit = 30 hours.
  • Integrated (theory + lab) courses: MVE212IA, MVE213IA, MVE221IA and MVE222IA print "CIE 50 Marks / SEE 50 Marks" on their course pages, but the scheme tables give 100 theory + 50 lab for both CIE and SEE, and the rubric totals 150. This site uses the scheme tables.
  • MBT325DB Clinical Data Management: the course page repeats the Nature Impelled Engineering units, outcomes and references (with one extra line on the Flectofin façade). The real CDM syllabus is not printed. The project cards for this course follow the course title.
  • Interdisciplinary electives: the scheme table lists a single row "XXX325DX Interdisciplinary Elective Course" with BoS "BT", and the index heads the list of 13 courses "GROUP-A". This site calls it the Interdisciplinary Elective.
  • MVE231TA UVM: the Unit II and Unit III headings appear swapped relative to their content (Unit II is headed "Coverage-Driven Verification and Register Modeling" but lists sequences, drivers, monitors and the factory; Unit III is headed "UVM Components, Sequences and Factory Mechanism" but lists coverage, scoreboards and RAL). Units IV and V share one heading, and references 1 and 3 are the same book. The unit summaries here follow the content, not the headings.
  • MVE222IA: the course page has no reference list; the title is printed "Systemverilog for Verification".
  • MVE332CB Healthcare SoCs: CO2 ("Design, synthesize, and implement combinational and sequential digital systems on FPGA platforms") is copied from the FPGA course.
  • MVE224BB RFIC Design: Unit V is headed "Communication through AWGN Channels contd" but covers frequency synthesis, power amplifiers and radio architectures.
  • MVE224BD Software Defined Vehicles: CO4 mentions sensor fusion and ADAS APIs, which are not in any unit.
  • MVE213IA: 13 experiments are listed; experiments 2 and 3 describe the same external-interrupt task.
  • MVE341ED: the title is printed "Phased Locked Loop". MEE325DG is now "Electric Vehicle Technology" (the 2024 title had "- II").
  • MVE435N: the title is "Industry Internship/research Internship/ Projects in Coes"; the scheme table categorises it as Lab, the course page as Professional Core Course.
  • Projects: the minor project (MVE434P) and internship (MVE435N) are 0:0:5 (5 credits) each and the major project (MVE442PA) is 0:0:5 (5 credits), though the major project is still an 18-week individual project with mandatory presentation or publication.
  • MOOC courses (MIM426RT, Group D, Group E) have no CIE; the SEE is the NPTEL examination. MVE341EB and MVE341ED list 12 weeks, MVE341EC lists 8.
  • Cover page: the PDF cover reads "Scheme And Syllabus Of I & IV Semester" although it contains Semesters I to IV.
  • Semester I timetable vs scheme (timetable w.e.f. 28.10.2026): see the Timetable page for the code differences (MVE2121A, MVE2131A, MVE216TA/B) and the Skill Lab that is not in the 2026 scheme.

Total entries: 49, counting each elective option separately.

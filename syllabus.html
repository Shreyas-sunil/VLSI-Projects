Syllabus

The RVCE M.Tech VLSI Design & Embedded Systems 2026 scheme, organised by semester and course, with the full syllabus text from the PDF and links to the project cards.

Programme outcomes

  1. PO1. Independently carry out research/investigation and development work to solve practical problems related to VLSI Design and Embedded Systems.
  2. PO2. Write and present a substantial technical report/document in the field of VLSI Design and Embedded Systems.
  3. PO3. Demonstrate a degree of mastery over the area of VLSI Design and Embedded Systems, at a level higher than the requirements of the bachelor's in Electronics & Communication Engineering.
  4. PO4. Abstract the requirements of modern microelectronics and smart systems to offer innovative solutions with available IPs and interfaces.
  5. PO5. Design and develop VLSI and Embedded modules with good economics to meet Quality of Service.
  6. PO6. Acquire professional and intellectual integrity, research ethics and execute socio-concern projects related to modern VLSI and Embedded Systems.

MVE311TBStatic Timing Analysis with TCL Scripting CoreL:T:P 3:1:0 (4)Theory

CIE marks: 100 · SEE marks: 100 (from the scheme tables)

Units at a glance

I Delay concepts, timing parameters, time borrowing, STA environment, virtual clocks · II Libraries, parasitics, delay models, clock-network optimisation, post-silicon timing, Tcl basics and design queries · III Noise and crosstalk, Tcl for crosstalk analysis · IV SDC constraints and exceptions, Tcl path tracing · V Violations, slack, timing checks and fixes, Tcl report analysis

Course outcomes

  • CO1: Apply static timing analysis concepts to evaluate timing behavior and identify critical timing paths in digital VLSI circuits.
  • CO2: Analyze the impact of parasitics, clock networks, variability, and crosstalk on circuit timing perfor- mance.
  • CO3: Develop and apply timing constraints to perform static timing analysis and validate timing require- ments using industry-standard EDA tools.
  • CO4: Evaluate timing reports, identify timing violations, and recommend optimization techniques to achieve timing closure.

MVE212IALow Power Digital IC Design CoreL:T:P 3:0:1 (4)Theory + Lab

CIE marks: 100+50 · SEE marks: 100+50 (from the scheme tables)

Units at a glance

I CMOS logic families, energy and power, sources of dissipation · II Delay models, logical effort, adders, circuit-level low power · III Latches, registers, low-power sequential design, encoding, gating · IV Memory arrays, interconnect parasitics, clock skew and jitter · V Architecture-level low power, voltage scaling, low-power clock distribution

Practicals / experiments

  1. CMOS Inverter: Static and Dynamic characteristics
  2. Delay & Power calculation of NAND/NOR gate
  3. Pre and Post Layout Power and Delay calculation of INX4 Inverter
  4. Pre and Post Layout simulation of NAND/NOR Gate
  5. Analyze the impact of Process, Voltage, and Temperature (PVT) Variations on the Delay and Power of an AOI22 Logic Gate
  6. Calculate delay and Power of 4 bit parallel adder
  7. Draw and Implement a master-slave D flip-flop
  8. Verify the Functional Correctness of a Digital Design through Simulation and Testbench Development
  9. Perform Code Coverage Analysis to Evaluate the Verification Completeness of a Digital Design
  10. Synthesize the given RTL Design and generate a netlist, report of area, power, and timing
  11. Perform Static Timing Analysis (STA) of the Synthesized Design and Analyze the Critical Path, Worst- Case Delay, Cell Utilization and power
  12. Verify functional equivalence between RTL and gate-level netlist using Logic Equivalence Checking (LEC)

Course outcomes

  • CO1: Investigate device, circuit & system aspects of Low power digital IC design
  • CO2: Analyze the functionality of digital integrated circuits & systems
  • CO3: Design and power estimation of digital integrated circuit & systems
  • CO4: Evaluate the performance parameters of a digital integrated circuits & systems

Units at a glance

I Embedded architectures, Cortex-M/A, RISC-V, DSP, memory hierarchy, benchmarking · II AMBA buses, PCIe/USB/MIPI, memories, timers, interrupts, DMA, power management · III Memory-mapped I/O, UART/SPI/I2C/I3C, CAN/CAN FD/LIN, FPGA, SoC/MPSoC · IV Drivers, BSP, HAL, CMSIS, toolchains, linker scripts, ELF, bootloaders, secure boot, FOTA, MISRA C, ISO 26262, AUTOSAR · V RTOS, FreeRTOS, CMSIS-RTOS v2, TinyML (STM32Cube.AI, TFLite Micro), PID control

  2. Implement an External Interrupt Handler Using Register-Level Programming for Push-Button-Based LED Control.
  3. Write an interrupt handler for an external interrupt using register-level programming to toggle an LED on an external push button event.
  4. Implement an ADC Driver Using STM32F407VG HAL and Interface an Analog Sensor.
  5. Implement an FIR Filter on the STM32F4 Cortex-M4 Using CMSIS-DSP and Test Its Response for Different Input Frequencies.
  6. Configure SPI in Full-Duplex Master Mode and Interface the LIS302DL 3-Axis Accelerometer.
  7. Implement Multitasking Using FreeRTOS with Concurrent LED Blinking Tasks.
  8. Deploy a TinyML-Based Temperature Prediction and Anomaly Detection Model on STM32F407VG.
  9. Deploy a TinyML-Based Sound Classification Model on STM32F407VG for Real-Time Audio Event Detection.
  10. Implement FreeRTOS Multitasking with Task Creation and Parameter Passing.
  11. Implement Task Synchronization Using FreeRTOS Mutexes or Semaphores for Shared USART Access.
  12. Implement Task Synchronization Using FreeRTOS Event Flags for Stepper Motor Control.
  13. Implement Inter-Process Communication Using a FreeRTOS Mailbox for Accelerometer Data Transfer.

Course outcomes

  • CO1: Illustrate embedded system architectures, processor organizations, memory hierarchy, and hardware- software co-design principles.
  • CO2: Analyze embedded hardware platforms, buses, memory subsystems, peripherals, and communication interfaces for real-world applications.
  • CO3: Develop reliable embedded software with device drivers, BSP, HAL, bootloaders, secure boot, and real-time kernel services.
  • CO4: Evaluate embedded systems that integrate hardware, RTOS, and communication protocols under performance, reliability, and resource constraints.

Motivation for SoC Design:
Introduction to SoC, SoB, SiP, Benefits of system-on-chip integration in terms of cost, power, and perfor-

System Design process, System level design issues- Soft IP vs Hard IP, Design for timing closure.

SoC Architecture and Components:
SoC architecture: bus-based, network-on-chip (NoC), and hybrid architectures, Components: processors,
memories, peripherals, and interfaces. Design considerations: power consumption, area, and performance.
Examples: SoC architectures for mobile devices, IoT, and automotive applications.
                                   Unit - III                                           9 Hours
Design and Verification of SoC:
Design languages: VHDL, Verilog, and SystemC, Design tools: synthesis, simulation, and verification,
Verification methodologies: simulation-based, formal verification, and emulation, Debugging and testing:
scan chains, built-in self-test (BIST), and boundary scan.

SOC Implementation:
Study of processor IP, Memory IP, wrapper Design - Real-time Operating system (RTOS), Peripheral
interface and components, High density FPGAs - EDA tools used for SOC design . Emerging trends: neu-
romorphic computing, quantum computing, and SoC security, Future directions: SoC design for emerging
applications.

Introduction to Healthcare SoC:
AI-powered healthcare SoCs, Neuromorphic computing for medical diagnostics, Integration with cloud and
edge computing, Threat modeling for medical devices, Real-time signal acquisition and compression.

CO3: Apply testing and verification methodologies to ensure the functionality and reliability of SoCs.
CO4: Analyze and optimize SoC designs for performance, power consumption and area.
